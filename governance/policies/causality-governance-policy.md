# Causality Governance Policy

## Purpose

Operational systems are shaped by chains of events, decisions, mutations, and recoveries.

Without causality awareness, organizations lose the ability to explain:

- why systems changed
- how failures emerged
- what mutations introduced drift
- what actions restored continuity

This policy defines how causality should be preserved within ARI.

---

# Core Principle

Operational history should be explainable as a chain of causally related events.

---

# Causality Examples

Examples:

- a prompt generated a specification
- a specification produced a deployment
- a deployment introduced drift
- a rollback restored continuity
- a governance review promoted trust

---

# Minimum Causality Expectations

Meaningful operational events should preserve:

- initiating actor
- affected artifacts
- related snapshots
- resulting trust transitions
- related runtime systems
- resulting operational states

---

# AI Governance Doctrine

AI systems should:

- preserve mutation ancestry
- preserve event lineage
- avoid anonymous operational mutation
- preserve rollback causality

---

# Relationship to Mutation Journaling

Mutation journaling records events.

Causality governance explains how events relate to each other.

---

# Future Direction

ARI may evolve toward:

- causality graph traversal
- operational replay systems
- governance simulation
- mutation impact propagation
- semantic event reasoning

---

# Operational Philosophy

Systems become difficult to govern when operational history loses causality.
