# Semantic Continuity Policy

## Purpose

Operational systems do not fail only because code breaks.

They also fail when meaning drifts.

This policy establishes the importance of semantic continuity within AI-assisted operational systems.

---

# Definition

Semantic continuity is the preservation of operational meaning across:

- implementations
- deployments
- AI systems
- documentation
- governance layers
- runtime evolution

---

# Examples of Semantic Drift

Examples:

- a metric changes definition without governance updates
- a prompt changes business meaning
- an implementation diverges from LST intent
- a deployment preserves behavior but changes operational assumptions
- an AI system rewrites logic using different semantics

---

# Core Principle

Operational continuity requires preserving not only structure, but meaning.

---

# Semantic Governance Expectations

Meaningful operational artifacts should:

- preserve intent
- preserve definitions
- preserve invariant meaning
- identify superseded semantics
- explain meaningful divergence

---

# Relationship to LST

The Logic Source of Truth layer governs intended operational meaning.

ARI exists partly to preserve continuity between:

- intended meaning
- implemented meaning
- deployed meaning
- historical meaning

---

# AI Mutation Doctrine

AI systems may unintentionally introduce semantic drift when:

- simplifying logic
- restructuring systems
- regenerating implementations
- replacing prompts
- optimizing aggressively

Therefore:

- semantic continuity should be reviewed
- lineage should be preserved
- operational meaning should remain explainable

---

# Future Direction

ARI may evolve toward:

- semantic diffing
- invariant comparison
- meaning-preserving mutation analysis
- AI semantic drift detection
- operational ontology mapping

---

# Operational Philosophy

A system can preserve syntax while losing meaning.

Semantic continuity preserves trust.
