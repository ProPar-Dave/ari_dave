# Governance Health Policy

## Purpose

Operational governance quality should become measurable.

This policy defines how governance health should be understood within ARI.

---

# Core Principle

A system may appear operationally healthy while governance quality silently deteriorates.

Examples:

- rollback coverage missing
- lineage incomplete
- runtime drift unknown
- semantic continuity broken
- trust boundaries unclear

Governance health exists partly to surface those hidden risks.

---

# Governance Health Dimensions

## Rollback Coverage

Measures:

- snapshot presence
- rollback playbooks
- recovery confidence
- known-good state references

---

## Lineage Coverage

Measures:

- provenance visibility
- dependency traceability
- mutation ancestry
- artifact relationships

---

## Drift Visibility

Measures:

- runtime comparison capability
- divergence awareness
- explainability of differences

---

## Invariant Coverage

Measures:

- documented invariants
- semantic preservation
- invariant traceability

---

## Trust Boundary Visibility

Measures:

- explicit trust classification
- promotion explainability
- reviewed versus inferred separation

---

# AI Governance Doctrine

AI systems should:

- preserve governance visibility
- avoid reducing recoverability
- avoid weakening lineage
- preserve operational explainability

---

# Future Direction

ARI may evolve toward:

- governance scoring
- drift risk analysis
- trust topology analysis
- operational resilience metrics
- semantic continuity scoring

---

# Operational Philosophy

Technical functionality alone is not sufficient evidence of operational health.
