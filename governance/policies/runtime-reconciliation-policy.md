# Runtime Reconciliation Policy

## Purpose

Operational systems naturally evolve and drift.

Over time, governed expectations and runtime reality may diverge.

This policy defines how reconciliation should be understood within ARI.

---

# Core Principle

Reconciliation is the process of restoring explainable alignment between:

- governed intent
- operational meaning
- runtime behavior
- deployment state
- trust expectations

---

# Reconciliation Examples

Examples:

- restoring drifted runtime behavior
- aligning deployment state with canonical artifacts
- resolving semantic divergence
- restoring lineage continuity
- rebuilding trust after operational mutation

---

# Reconciliation Expectations

Meaningful reconciliation should preserve:

- operational continuity
- lineage explainability
- rollback visibility
- semantic continuity
- trust transition visibility

---

# AI Governance Doctrine

AI systems should:

- identify potential runtime drift
- preserve reconciliation explainability
- avoid destructive reconciliation when possible
- preserve prior state visibility
- preserve recovery optionality

---

# Relationship to Recovery

Recovery restores a trusted prior state.

Reconciliation restores explainable alignment.

These are related but distinct concepts.

---

# Future Direction

ARI may evolve toward:

- automated drift comparison
- semantic reconciliation analysis
- causality-aware recovery planning
- topology-aware runtime alignment
- governance-aware orchestration

---

# Operational Philosophy

Operational systems become fragile when runtime reality diverges silently from governed intent.
