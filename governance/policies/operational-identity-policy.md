# Operational Identity Policy

## Purpose

Operational systems evolve continuously.

Without stable identity, systems lose:

- lineage continuity
- dependency traceability
- causality explainability
- rollback coherence
- trust continuity

This policy defines how operational identity should be preserved within ARI.

---

# Core Principle

Operational entities may evolve.

Their identity should remain explainable across that evolution.

---

# Identity Categories

## Artifact Identity

Examples:

- edge functions
- prompts
- policies
- registries
- runtime snapshots

Artifacts should preserve stable identifiers across versions.

---

## Runtime Identity

Examples:

- Supabase environments
- deployment environments
- orchestration runtimes

Runtime systems should preserve explainable operational identity.

---

## Event Identity

Examples:

- mutation events
- rollback events
- promotion events
- recovery events

Events should preserve causal traceability.

---

## Invariant Identity

Examples:

- semantic rules
- operational assumptions
- canonical definitions

Invariant identity should survive implementation changes.

---

# AI Governance Doctrine

AI systems should:

- preserve stable identity references
- avoid silently replacing operational identity
- preserve lineage continuity
- preserve causality explainability

---

# Future Direction

ARI may evolve toward:

- identity resolution systems
- semantic entity graphs
- runtime ancestry reconstruction
- causality-aware governance reasoning
- operational digital twins

---

# Operational Philosophy

Systems lose continuity when identity becomes ambiguous.
