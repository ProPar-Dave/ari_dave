# Recovery Versus Regeneration Policy

## Purpose

Modern AI systems can regenerate many lost artifacts.

However, regeneration is not equivalent to operational recovery.

This policy formalizes that distinction.

---

# Core Principle

Recovery preserves operational continuity.

Regeneration recreates approximations.

Those are not the same thing.

---

# What Recovery Preserves

Recovery attempts to preserve:

- operational lineage
- historical context
- deployment assumptions
- runtime behavior
- semantic continuity
- validation history
- rollback ancestry
- trusted state references

---

# What Regeneration Often Loses

Regeneration may lose:

- exact operational behavior
- historical reasoning
- deployment nuance
- hidden dependencies
- runtime assumptions
- semantic invariants
- prior validation state

Even when regenerated systems appear correct.

---

# AI Governance Doctrine

AI systems should:

- prefer preservation over reconstruction
- avoid assuming regeneration equals restoration
- preserve snapshots before mutation
- maintain lineage whenever possible

---

# Runtime Doctrine

The existence of source files alone does not guarantee recoverability.

Operational recovery may also require:

- runtime configuration
- deployment topology
- environment assumptions
- policy definitions
- migration history
- trusted snapshots

---

# Relationship to ARI

ARI exists partly to reduce dependence on regeneration.

ARI prioritizes:

- preservation
- explainability
- rollback confidence
- operational continuity
- lineage-aware recovery

---

# Future Direction

ARI may evolve toward:

- recovery simulation
- lineage-aware restoration
- operational replay
- trust-preserving rollback
- semantic recovery analysis

---

# Operational Philosophy

A regenerated system may look similar.

A recovered system preserves continuity.
