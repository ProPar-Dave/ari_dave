# Invariant Governance Policy

## Purpose

Operational systems require stable truths.

Without explicit invariants, systems become vulnerable to:

- semantic drift
- inconsistent implementations
- AI reinterpretation
- governance fragmentation
- operational instability

This policy establishes how invariants should be governed within ARI.

---

# Definition

An invariant is a rule, assumption, or meaning that should remain stable across:

- implementations
- deployments
- environments
- AI systems
- architectural evolution

---

# Types of Invariants

## Semantic Invariants

Examples:

- metric definitions
- business meaning
- calculation doctrine
- canonical terminology

---

## Structural Invariants

Examples:

- identifier requirements
- lineage preservation expectations
- immutable snapshot doctrine
- promotion constraints

---

## Operational Invariants

Examples:

- rollback-before-mutation
- no secret storage
- runtime explainability
- canonical review requirements

---

# Core Principle

Systems may evolve.

Invariants preserve continuity across that evolution.

---

# AI Governance Doctrine

AI systems may:

- restructure implementations
- optimize systems
- regenerate artifacts
- rewrite operational code

AI systems should not silently violate governed invariants.

---

# Invariant Preservation Expectations

Meaningful operational mutations should:

- preserve declared invariants
- explicitly identify invariant changes
- justify invariant modifications
- preserve lineage to prior invariant definitions

---

# Invariant Registry Expectations

Important invariants should eventually be registered with:

- identifier
- category
- status
- governing authority
- dependent artifacts
- related runtime systems

---

# Relationship to LST

The Logic Source of Truth layer defines many semantic invariants.

ARI exists partly to preserve those invariants through:

- deployment evolution
- runtime mutation
- AI-assisted implementation
- organizational change

---

# Future Direction

ARI may evolve toward:

- invariant validation
- semantic invariant diffing
- runtime invariant enforcement
- AI invariant violation detection
- ontology-aware operational governance

---

# Operational Philosophy

Without explicit invariants, systems eventually lose coherence.
